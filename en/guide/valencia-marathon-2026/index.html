Welcome to Valencia

The record-breaking marathon

The Maratón Valencia Trinidad Alfonso Zurich, organised by S.D. Correcaminos, reaches its 46th edition and holds the World Athletics Platinum Label, the highest recognition for a road race. Up to 36,000 runners from all over the world line up, and the organiser calls it the fastest and flattest circuit in the world: for 2026 the stated goal is the women's world record.

The course is almost perfectly flat, barely 16 metres between its lowest and highest points. It starts on the Pont de Montolivet, next to the City of Arts and Sciences, heads straight for the sea and the university district, loops through the northern neighbourhoods, returns to the coast through the Cabanyal, enters the old town under the Torres de Serranos, makes a wide loop west to the Bioparc and finishes on the blue walkway floating over the lake of the City of Arts, in front of the Hemisfèric.

In this guide you will find the practical information for the weekend: hotel, KiRun support, the Saturday with us, bib collection, start corrals, course, after the race and a mini guide to the city. Times and rules come from the official website (2026 regulations, Expo page, 2026 course map and split times), checked on 22 November. Getting to Valencia

Getting there

You travel to Valencia independently, arriving on Friday 4 December (or earlier, if you want to visit the Expo on Thursday evening). Valencia airport is west of the city, close to the fair where you collect your bib.

From the airport to the Expo and the hotel

  • Go to the Expo straight away: Feria Valencia is about ten minutes by taxi from the airport. Collect your bib and bag in peace, then head to the hotel.
  • Taxi: from the arrivals rank, 20–25 minutes to the hotel; the easiest option with luggage.
  • Metro: lines 3 or 5 from Aeroport station to Xàtiva (about 25 minutes), then walk to the nearby Alacant station and take line 10 to Oceanogràfic, a few minutes' walk from the hotel. Tickets from the machines or with a contactless card at the gates.
  • Leaving: check-out by 12:00 on Monday 7; luggage can be left at reception. Allow 20–25 minutes by taxi to the airport, more at rush hour.

Collecting your bib

  • Bring an original ID document and keep your registration confirmation email handy. If the organiser sends a QR code for collection (for the October half marathon it arrived by email on the Thursday before the race), check your spam folder too; if nothing has arrived in the days before you leave, write to us.

Sunday 6 December

Race day

Both start and finish are at the City of Arts and Sciences: you start on the Pont de Montolivet and finish on the blue walkway over the lake, in front of the Hemisfèric. The corrals line up behind the start; bag drop, medal engraving and the post-finish area are on Avinguda del Professor López Piñero, next to the Umbracle.

From the hotel to your corral

The Holiday Inn Express Ciudad de las Ciencias is in a prime location: you can stay at the hotel until a few minutes before entering your corral. There is no bag drop for you, but your room is probably closer to the corral entrance and to the post-race area than the bag drop itself.

We will show you how to reach your corral, about 300 metres from the hotel, and the way back to the hotel on foot, about 400 metres from the runners' exit. You will not need any transport.

Bag drop

The official bag drop (6 €, 8,000 places) had to be booked online by 1 November and is sold out: it cannot be bought on race day. If you booked it, your bib shows a coat-hanger icon; it is on Avinguda del Professor López Piñero, next to the Umbracle, one backpack or cabin bag per runner. From the Holiday Inn Express you do not need it: leave everything in your room.

Start corrals and pacers

The start is split into nine waves, from 8:15 to 9:35, and your corral depends on the time accredited at registration: it is printed on your bib. Go to your own corral: you may move back, never forward. The official time is net time, from when you cross the start line. The MSC pacers («Prácticos») run with a highly visible banner showing their finish time: get behind the one for your target and let them carry you. The time limit is 5 hours 30 minutes from when you cross the start line; anyone passing km 25 after 12:57 must hand in their bib and leave the course.

Race-eve checklist
  • Bib on the front of your top with four safety pins, chip on the back: do not fold it. On the back write your emergency number, medical conditions and blood group.
  • Shoes and socks you have already trained in. At dawn in early December Valencia is chilly: an old layer to throw off before the start.
  • Gels or fuel you have already tested: on the course there are water and Aquarius at every aid station and Enervit gels at km 16.2, 25.8 and 34.
  • Charged phone and an ID, unless you leave them at the hotel.
  • On Sunday morning you stay in your room until a few minutes before entering your corral, about 300 metres away: we will show you the way.
42.195 km

The course

The official distance is 42.195 km on an almost flat course: the lowest point is 1.8 metres above sea level, the highest 18 metres, 16.2 metres of elevation difference in total. Wide roads and few bends. The references below come from the official 2026 split-time table and the course map. The start and finish are a few minutes' walk from your hotel: whoever is with you can see you start and finish.

  • km 0–4 · From the Pont de Montolivet straight towards the sea: Avinguda de Balears, the port and the Marina de València, then north behind the beach to Calle Eugenia Viñes, in Malvarrosa.
  • km 5–8 · Avinguda dels Tarongers, the big avenue of the university district, inland to Plaza Emilio Attard.
  • km 9–13 · The northern loop: Avinguda de Catalunya, Ronda Nord, Avinguda d'Alfahuir, then south again along Calle Dolores Marqués and Calle General Elio to the Jardins del Real (Viveros).
  • km 14–20 · Paseo de la Alameda, Avinguda d'Aragó next to the Mestalla stadium, Avinguda de Blasco Ibáñez and Calle Ramón Llull, then Avinguda dels Tarongers again towards the sea. First Enervit gel at km 16.2.
  • km 21–25 · Halfway is in the Cabanyal, on Calle de la Reina, the seafaring quarter with its tiled façades; then Avinguda del Port (km 22–23), Calle Eduardo Boscà and Plaza Zaragoza. First rapid assistance point at km 24.5, the 12:57 cut-off at km 25.
  • km 26–31 · Towards the centre: second gel at km 25.8, the Alameda again, Calle Guadalaviar and Calle Blanquerías, under the Torres de Serranos (km 28), then the heart of the old town between Calle Pintor López and Calle Poeta Querol, and Calle Guillem de Castro (km 31).
  • km 32–38 · The wide western loop: Gran Via Fernando el Católico, Calle Valle de la Ballestera next to the Bioparc (km 33–34, third gel at km 34), Avinguda Tres Cruces, Calle Archiduque Carlos and Calle San José de Calasanz. This is where the marathon really starts: focus on your pace and the aid stations.
  • km 39–42 · The way back: Calle Xàtiva past the Estació del Nord and the bullring, Porta de la Mar (km 40), Avinguda Jacinto Benavente and the City of Arts and Sciences. The last metres are on the blue walkway over the lake, in front of the Hemisfèric.
  • Aid stations at km 5.5 · 10 · 14.5 · 18.7 · 23 · 28.3 · 33.1 · 37.1 · 40.4 and at the finish, with Aquabona water in 33 cl bottles and Aquarius in cups; fruit too from km 23. Every aid station has toilets and medical staff.
  • Enervit gels, with and without caffeine, at km 16.2 · 25.8 · 34 (Red Points). Rapid assistance by Farmacia Ribera, with vaseline and pain-relief spray, at km 24.5 · 29.5 · 34.2 and after the finish.
  • Split-time table: the official PDF gives, for every kilometre, the passing time of the first and last runners. Send it to whoever is coming to watch: they will know when to look for you.
  • Photos: Marathon Photos shoots along the course; search by bib number on marathonphotos.live, where you can also download your certificate for free.

After the finish line

The finish and after the race

  • Keep walking past the line: in the post-finish area you receive your medal (for everyone finishing within the time limit) and the finisher's bag with an Enervit protein bar, Aquabona water, Aquarius, Canary Island bananas and persimmon.

Beyond the race

Mini guide to Valencia

Getting around

  • Metro and tram (Metrovalencia): from the hotel, line 10 from Oceanogràfic takes you to Alacant in the centre in about ten minutes; from there, Xàtiva station for the other lines. Pay with a contactless card at the gates or with the rechargeable card.
  • EMT buses: a dense network, pay on board with a contactless card; the EMT València app shows real-time times.
  • On foot and by bike: the old town is best on foot; the Turia Gardens link the City of Arts to the centre in a 30–40 minute walk, and the city is full of cycle lanes (Valenbisi bike sharing).
  • Taxis at the ranks and through apps (Cabify, Uber, FREE NOW); fares are reasonable. On Sunday morning many streets around the course are closed.

Five places not to miss

City of Arts and Sciences

Valencia's modern landmark, designed by Santiago Calatrava, five minutes from the hotel: the Palau de les Arts, the Hemisfèric, the Science Museum, the Umbracle and the Oceanogràfic, Europe's largest aquarium. Worth seeing at night too, lit up.

Old town

The Cathedral with the Miguelete tower (207 steps and the best view of the city), Plaza de la Virgen, the UNESCO-listed Lonja de la Seda and the medieval Serranos and Quart towers. Saturday's walking tour is the right way to start.

Mercado Central

One of Europe's largest covered markets, in a 1928 modernist building: over a thousand stalls of fruit, fish, jamón and spices. Closed on Sundays: the right place for breakfast the day after the race.

Turia Gardens

Nine kilometres of park in the old riverbed, from Cabecera Park to the City of Arts: where we run together on Saturday morning and the right place for a recovery walk after the race.

Malvarrosa and Cabanyal

The city beach with its fine sand and the seafaring quarter with tiled façades, where you pass at halfway: after the marathon, a seaside lunch on the Paseo Marítimo is the right way to end the weekend.

Three dishes and a drink

  • Paella valenciana: the original, with chicken, rabbit, green beans and garrofó, born right here. Eaten at lunch, never in the evening: perfect on Sunday after the race.
  • Esgarraet: roasted peppers, salt cod and olive oil, the Valencian tapa par excellence.
  • Fideuà: the short-noodle «paella» with seafood, born in the port district you run through in the first kilometres.
  • Horchata with fartons: the cold tiger-nut drink with long sweet buns for dipping. Try it in a historic horchatería in the centre.
Before you leave

Practical information for Spain

Documents
EU citizens need a valid ID card or passport, also required at the Expo to collect your bib. Other nationalities: check the entry requirements for Spain.
Currency
Euro. Contactless cards are accepted everywhere, including metro and buses: you need very little cash.
Language
Spanish and Valencian (signs are bilingual). English is understood in tourist areas.
Electricity
Type C/F sockets, 230 V. Travellers from the UK, Ireland and outside continental Europe need an adapter.
Phone
Country code +34. Roaming within the EU is usually included in EU plans: check with your provider if you come from elsewhere.
Time zone
Central European Time (UTC+1), the same as Italy and one hour ahead of the UK.
Weather
In early December Valencia's days are usually mild and often sunny, but early mornings are chilly. On Sunday 6 the sun rises around 8:05 and sets around 17:40: it is just light at the first start, and the 17:00 photo at the Umbracle falls in the sunset light.
Public holidays
Sunday 6 December is Constitution Day and Tuesday 8 is the Immaculate Conception, a national holiday: many shops are closed on those days.
Emergencies
112 for police, ambulance and fire brigade. With a European Health Insurance Card (EHIC/GHIC) you are entitled to treatment in the Spanish public health service. Pharmacies are marked with a green cross, many open 24 hours.
Water and tips
Tap water is safe but very hard: most people drink bottled water. Tipping is not compulsory; leave a few euros if you liked the service.
