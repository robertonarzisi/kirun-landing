Welcome to Valencia

The fastest half marathon in the world

The Valencia Half Marathon Trinidad Alfonso Zurich, organised by S.D. Correcaminos, reaches its 35th edition and is the half marathon where world records fall: Letesenbet Gidey ran 1:02:52 here and Yomif Kejelcha 57:30. It holds the World Athletics Gold Label, it is a stage of the SuperHalfs series (your result counts towards your SuperPassport) and it brings around 25,000 runners from all over the world to the start line, with bibs allocated by ballot.

The course is flat and smooth, with few turns and just 16.7 metres between its lowest and highest points: it starts on Avinguda dels Tarongers, in the university district, brushes past the Horta Nord, follows the upper side of the Turia Gardens, enters the old town between the medieval towers and the Central Market, passes in front of the City of Arts and Sciences (a stone's throw from your hotel), reaches the Marina and returns to the finish through the seaside district of El Cabanyal. Mild weather, music and more than sixty entertainment points along the way.

In this guide you will find the practical information to organise your weekend: hotel, bib collection, start boxes, course, what happens after the finish and a mini guide to the city. Times are those published by the organiser as of 13 October; the official Race Guide is not out yet and will arrive in the days before the race. Always check the official links in the side panel.

The weekend at a glance

The four-day programme

  1. Fri 23
    Arrival and bib collection

    From the airport to the hotel by taxi (20–25 minutes) or by metro with one change. Rooms are ready from 16:00; if you arrive earlier, leave your luggage at reception.

    Bibs are collected at the Expo 21K at Feria Valencia, open from 9:00 to 21:00. To save time, you can go straight from the airport to the Expo by taxi (about 10 minutes: the exhibition centre is close to the airport) and then carry on to the hotel. Avoid 18:00–20:00, the busiest slot. From 18:00 to 19:00 the KiRun staff are in the lobby: we meet each other and you get the latest information.

  2. Sat 24
    Shake-out and race eve

    Morning: 20–30 minutes of easy running with a few strides in the Turia Gardens, which begin five minutes from the hotel. If you do not have your bib yet, the Expo is open from 9:00 to 21:00: avoid 12:00–14:00 and 18:00–20:00. The Expo also hosts La Placeta, with running talks and the presentation of the elite athletes.

    From 18:00 to 19:00 the staff are in the lobby for the race-eve briefing, Sunday's transfer time and your last questions. Then an early, carb-based dinner and get your bib, wristband and kit ready. Tonight daylight saving time ends: at 3:00 the clocks go back to 2:00 and you get an extra hour of sleep.

  3. Sun 25
    Race day

    Breakfast at the hotel from 5:30, then the KiRun transfer to the start area at the time given by our staff. Wave starts from 8:25 (boxes 1–3) to 9:27 (box 9), finish on Avinguda dels Tarongers and back to the hotel with the dedicated transfer. The afternoon is yours: paella, the beach or the City of Arts. From 18:00 to 19:00 the staff are in the lobby again to celebrate with you.

  4. Mon 26
    Journey home

    Check-out by 12:00. If your flight is in the afternoon, leave your luggage at reception and enjoy your last hours in the city. The staff remain available by phone all day.

Where you stay

The hotel and how to get there

You are staying at the Holiday Inn Express Valencia Ciudad de las Ciencias, Calle Escritor Rafael Ferreres 22, 46013 Valencia: a modern, practical three-star hotel five minutes' walk from the City of Arts and Sciences, with air-conditioned rooms, free Wi-Fi, buffet breakfast included and a seasonal outdoor pool. The area is quiet, with restaurants and cafés nearby and the Turia Gardens a few steps away for your shake-out run.

The race course passes right in front, around km 15–16, between the Palau de les Arts and the Oceanogràfic: the ideal spot for anyone coming to cheer you on.

From the airport to the hotel

  • Taxi: from the rank outside arrivals, 20–25 minutes to the hotel; the easiest option with luggage. If you want to collect your bib straight away, ask the driver to stop at Feria Valencia first (see the Expo section).

Check-in, check-out and breakfast

  • Check-in from 16:00, check-out by 12:00; before and after, luggage can be left at reception.

Before the race

The Expo 21K and bib collection

Everything starts at the Expo 21K, set up in Hall 5 of Feria Valencia, the exhibition centre north-west of the city: this is where you collect your bib, chip, runner's bag and the identification wristband you need to enter your start box. There are sponsor and brand stands, food areas and La Placeta, the space with free running talks and the presentation of the elite athletes.

Collecting your bib

  • Bring an original ID document and keep your registration confirmation on your phone (in past years the organiser emailed a QR code in the days before the race: check your spam folder too). If you cannot find anything, tell us right away.
  • With your bib you receive the wristband to enter your box and the runner's bag with sponsor products. The chip is on the back of the bib: the regulations ask you to check it at the chip-control stand at the Expo before you leave.
  • Cannot make it to the Expo? Collection by a third party is possible: from 1 October, in the Runner's Area, you can authorise another person by entering their details. If you need this, contact us: we will tell you who to authorise.
  • Improved your time? You can change box in the Runner's Area until 18 October for a €25 fee; after that no changes are possible.
  • If you come to the Expo with your luggage straight from the airport: there is a paid left-luggage service at the entrance to Hall 5 (cash only), so you can walk around the Expo hands-free and then carry on to the hotel.
  • Bring four safety pins from home for your bib.
Sunday 25 October

Race day

The clocks change tonight

On the night between Saturday 24 and Sunday 25 October daylight saving time ends: at 3:00 the clocks go back to 2:00, in Spain as in the rest of the EU and the UK. You get an extra hour of sleep. Your phone updates by itself: make sure your alarm is set and do not rely on clocks that do not adjust automatically.

The race starts and finishes on Avinguda dels Tarongers, the wide avenue of the university district in the north-east of the city: the start is next to the Universitat Politècnica, the finish a little further on, next to the Universitat de València. It is about 4 km from the hotel: the dedicated KiRun transfer takes you to the start area and brings you back to the hotel after the finish.

Getting to the start

  • KiRun transfer: meet in the hotel lobby at the time we give you on Saturday evening, based on your box. Be on time: on race morning many roads are closed.
  • If you travel on your own, bear in mind that in past editions tram lines 4 and 6 in the university district did not run until midday and several buses were diverted: check the Metrovalencia and EMT notices on the Citizen info page.
  • The boxes open about an hour before the start (at 7:30 in 2025); you enter by showing the bib and wristband you received at the Expo. Anyone arriving after their box has closed starts with the following wave.

Bag drop

The bag drop at the start is reserved for those who booked it at registration (the service is sold out) and is marked with the hanger symbol on the bib. With the KiRun transfer you do not need it: leave everything in your room and take only the essentials. For the wait in the box an old sweatshirt to leave in the collection bins is fine.

Start boxes and pacers

The start is staggered in seven waves and your box, from 1 to 9, depends on the expected finish time you gave at registration: it is printed on your bib and matches the colour of your wristband. Line up in your own box: you may move back, never forward. Wheelchair athletes start early in the fourth wave; visually impaired runners may start at the front of their wave. The official time is net time: it starts when you cross the start line. Toilets in the start area, inside the boxes and at every aid station.

There are MSC pacers («Prácticos») in the race, recognisable by the flag with the finish time, for the main target times: line up behind yours and let them carry you. The time limit is 2 hours 45 minutes from your crossing of the start line; anyone reaching km 10 after 11:00 must hand in their bib and board the sweeper vehicle. The finish closes at 12:20.

Race-eve checklist
  • Bib pinned to the front of your shirt with four safety pins, chip integrated: do not fold it. Wristband on: without it you cannot enter the box.
  • Shoes and socks already worn in training; in late October it is 13–15 °C in the morning: an old layer to leave in the box and off you go.
  • Gels or nutrition you have already tested: on the course you will find water, Aquarius and Enervit gels at km 15.
  • ID card, payment card and a charged phone, unless you leave them at the hotel.
  • Alarm checked after the clock change; KiRun transfer time noted.
21.0975 km

The course

The official distance is 21.0975 km, certified by AIMS and the RFEA, on an almost perfectly flat course: the lowest point is 1.6 metres above sea level and the highest 18.3, with 16.7 metres of elevation difference in total. Few turns, wide roads and an anticlockwise loop that touches all the city's landmarks. The kilometres below are approximate, taken from the official 2026 map.

  • km 0–3 · Start on Avinguda dels Tarongers heading north, on the edge of the Horta Nord and its tiger-nut fields, then a turn inland by the Ciutat de València stadium.
  • km 3–5 · Long straights towards the centre past the Jardins del Real (Viveros) and the Fine Arts Museum; at km 5 the first aid station and, just after it, the Torres de Serranos, the city's medieval gate.
  • km 5–9 · The wide loop around the centre along the upper side of the Turia Gardens, between the Botanical Garden and Plaça d'Espanya: the most open stretch, where you settle into your rhythm.
  • km 9–13 · The historic heart: the Torres de Quart, the Cathedral and Palau de la Generalitat area, the Central Market and the Lonja, the bullring and the Estació del Nord. Aid station around km 10.
  • km 13–16 · From the Palau de la Música along the Turia to the City of Arts and Sciences: the Palau de les Arts, the Hemisfèric, the Science Museum and the Oceanogràfic. Here you find the Enervit Red Point with gels (km 15.2), fast assistance (km 15.1) and an aid station (km 15.5). Your hotel is a stone's throw away: the right spot for anyone waiting for you.
  • km 16–19 · Towards the sea through El Grau to the Marina de València with the Veles e Vents building, fast assistance at km 18.5 and the last aid station at km 19.4.
  • km 19–21 · Back through the streets of El Cabanyal, the old fishermen's district, and the final straight on Avinguda dels Tarongers to the «pasarela azul», the blue finish walkway.
  • Aid stations at km 5.4 · 10.4 · 15.5 · 19.4 with bottled Aquabona water and Aquarius in cups, toilets at every station; Enervit gels with and without caffeine at km 15.2; fast-assistance points with vaseline and pain-relief spray at km 15.1 and 18.5. At the finish water, Aquarius and fruit.
  • Photos, tracking and app: Marathon Photos shoots along the course and you search your photos by bib number on marathonphotos.live; with the official app (Medio Maratón Valencia, on the App Store and Google Play) your people at home follow you live and you download your certificate for free.
  • Crowds and music: bands, DJs, batucadas and falla groups all along the course, with the thickest crowds in the centre, at the City of Arts and on the final straight.

After the finish line

The finish and after the race

  • Keep walking past the line: in the post-finish area you receive your medal and the finisher's bag with Aquabona water, Aquarius, a banana, a persimmon and an Enervit bar. Do not stop right after the arch.
  • Medal engraving with your name and official time: in the bag-drop area, next to the start. If you bought it with your registration it is marked on your bib; otherwise you pay on the spot.
  • Showers for all runners from 10:00 to 14:00 at the Benimaclet sports centre, near the finish area. Farmacia Ribera also provides assistance in the post-finish area.
  • Back to the hotel with the dedicated KiRun transfer: our staff will give you the meeting point and time. If you need it, the hotel allows a late check-out until 16:00 on Sunday.
  • SuperHalfs: your Valencia result counts towards your SuperPassport; if it does not appear, you can claim it from your profile on superhalfs.com. If this is your sixth race, ask at the SuperHalfs stand at the Expo about collecting your SuperMedal.
  • Official results and certificate on the organiser's website and in the app; photos on marathonphotos.live.
  • The KiRun staff are waiting for you in the hotel lobby from 18:00 to 19:00: for a problem, a photo with your medal or just to tell us about your race.
Saturday 24 October

Saturday in Valencia

KiRun tip: on race eve 20–30 minutes of easy running with a few strides is enough. The right place is the Jardí del Túria, the 9 km park in the old riverbed, which begins right at the City of Arts: flat surface, shade and no traffic lights. The Malvarrosa seafront is a great alternative.

If you do not have your bib yet, the Expo is open until 21:00 (avoid 12:00–14:00 and 18:00–20:00); it is worth stopping at La Placeta for the talks and the elite presentation. Then an easy afternoon, an early, carb-based dinner (book ahead: restaurants are full on half-marathon Saturday; in Valencia paella is a lunch dish, not a dinner one) and get your bib, wristband and kit ready. Before bed, check your alarm: the clocks go back tonight.

Beyond the race

Mini guide to Valencia

Getting around

  • Metro and tram (Metrovalencia): from the hotel, line 10 from Oceanogràfic takes you to Alacant in the centre in about ten minutes; from there Xàtiva station serves the other lines. Pay with a contactless card at the gates or with a rechargeable travel card.
  • EMT buses: a dense network, pay on board with a contactless card; the EMT València app shows real-time timetables.
  • On foot and by bike: the old town is best explored on foot; the Turia Gardens link the City of Arts to the centre in a 30–40 minute walk, and the city is full of cycle paths (Valenbisi shared bikes).
  • Taxis at ranks and via apps (Cabify, Uber, FREE NOW); fares are reasonable.

Five places not to miss

City of Arts and Sciences

Valencia's modern landmark, designed by Santiago Calatrava, five minutes from the hotel: the Palau de les Arts, the Hemisfèric, the Science Museum, the Umbracle and the Oceanogràfic, Europe's largest aquarium. Worth seeing lit up at night too.

Old town

The Cathedral with the Miguelete tower (207 steps and the best view of the city), Plaza de la Virgen, the UNESCO-listed Lonja de la Seda and the medieval Serranos and Quart towers, which you will run past on race day.

Central Market

One of Europe's largest covered markets, in a 1928 modernist building: more than a thousand stalls of fruit, fish, jamón and spices. Perfect for Monday breakfast before your flight.

Turia Gardens

Nine kilometres of park in the old riverbed, from the Parque de Cabecera to the City of Arts: the right place for Saturday's shake-out run and Monday's stroll.

Malvarrosa and El Cabanyal

The city beach with its fine sand and the fishermen's district with tiled façades: after the race, lunch overlooking the sea on the Paseo Marítimo is the right way to round off the weekend.

Three dishes and a drink

  • Paella valenciana: the original one, with chicken, rabbit, green beans and garrofó beans, born right here. Eaten at lunch, never in the evening: perfect on Sunday after the race.
  • Esgarraet: roasted peppers, salt cod and olive oil, the Valencian tapa par excellence.
  • Fideuà: the short-pasta «paella» with seafood, born in the port district of El Grau, which you will run through at km 17.
  • Horchata with fartons: the cold drink made from tiger nuts, grown in the Horta Nord fields you brush past in the first kilometres, with the long sweet buns to dip. Try it in a historic horchatería in the centre or in Alboraia.
Before you leave

Practical information for Spain

Documents
EU citizens need only a valid identity card or passport; no visa or authorisation. Travellers from outside the EU (including the UK) need a passport: check the entry rules for your nationality before you travel. The organiser requires foreign runners to be over 18.
Currency
Euro. Contactless cards are accepted everywhere, including on the metro and buses: you need very little cash.
Language
Spanish and Valencian (signs are bilingual). English is understood in tourist areas.
Electricity
Type C/F sockets, 230 V: travellers from the UK and Ireland need an adapter; continental European plugs fit.
Phone
Country code +34. EU roaming applies for EU SIM cards; UK and other travellers should check their roaming plan or consider an eSIM.
Time zone
Central European Time (one hour ahead of the UK). Mind the clock change: on the night between Saturday 24 and Sunday 25 at 3:00 the clocks go back to 2:00, in Spain as in the UK.
Weather
In late October Valencia averages 14 to 23 °C: cool early in the morning, mild and often sunny during the day, with some chance of rain. On Sunday 25, after the clock change, the sun rises at about 7:30 and sets at about 18:10.
Emergencies
112 for police, ambulance and fire brigade. EU citizens with the EHIC card are entitled to public healthcare. Pharmacies are marked by a green cross, many open 24 hours.
Water and tipping
Tap water is drinkable but very hard: almost everyone drinks bottled water. Tipping in restaurants is not expected; leave a few euros if you enjoyed the service.
